## [Unreleased]

### Added

- A UI stack of our own, owning layout, drawing, input and motion end to end (Design/UIStack.md, 18)
- Reactive core: `Observable<T>`, `Computation` and `Effect`, run in three phases (model, layout, draw), earliest first, reporting a computation that keeps invalidating itself
- `UIValue<T>` for every property: a literal, a token or a binding, with variants for interaction states (Selected, Hovered, Focused, Pressed, Disabled, and FocusWithin, the weakest) and states of your own (`UIState`), and two faces: layout and game code read where a value is going, drawing reads where it is drawn
- `UI.Animate`: a change and everything its layout moves spring from where they are drawn, at the speed they have, a node made and changed in one go springing from where it was put; `UITransaction` says when it has finished and whether it completed
- Velocity: every value that interpolates (numbers, vectors, colours, rects, insets, corner radii) knows the speed it moves at (`AnimatedValue.Velocity`), on a spring, set by a gesture, or moved by a scroll dragged or gliding; a spring set off on a moving value starts at its speed; a value already moving that is set directly heads there keeping its speed, and one a gesture's callbacks set lands at once, under the finger
- Gestures hand their speed on, as SwiftUI's tracksVelocity: what the change callbacks of a `Draggable` or `Pinchable` set directly is sampled, the change made as it lets go starts at that speed, and once it is over what it moved that nothing took over lands still; `TracksVelocity` off for precise controls, such as a stepped slider whose snap must not overshoot
- Tokens (`ColorToken`, `FloatToken`, `Vector2Token`, `InsetsToken`, `CornerRadiiToken`) with a value per mode
- `UIRoot`, `UINode` (Clay's layout: fit, grow, fixed and percent sizes, padding, gaps, wrapping, alignment, aspect ratio, floating against the parent, the root or another node, the safe area) and the drawn values Offset, Scale, Rotation, Opacity, ZIndex, Clips and TopLayer
- Drawables: `Box` (rounded rect with per-corner radii, gradient, border, shadow), `Label` (UI.Text's layout and Markdown, SDF and colour glyphs), `ImageView` (sprites and textures, fill, contain or cover, rounded), and `UIDrawable` and `Painter` (shapes, images, glyphs and laid-out text) for drawables of your own
- A renderer of one instanced shader drawing from structured buffers in a URP render graph pass after the camera stack, in the Game and Scene views, clipping by up to four rounded rects, with up to eight textures a draw call
- Input from the Input System: pointers (mouse, pen, every finger) hit tested against what was drawn, `IPointerHandler`, `IHoverHandler` and `IScrollHandler`, `Pressable` (with long press), `Draggable` (saying which drags it takes, `Takes`), `Pinchable` (two-finger pinch and turn), `ScrollView` (rubber band, iOS deceleration, bounce, pages, wheel, indicators; a new drag heading past an end it is at, or past one it is caught bouncing back from, goes to a scroll view it is in that can still scroll that way; past an end a drag stretches it, or with `AtEnd` set to PassOn the rest of the drag goes to a scroll view it is in that can go on, or a `Draggable` that takes it, which hands it back as the finger turns, as a sheet and the list in it share a drag on iOS; and the wheel at an end scrolls the scroll view it is in), `UIFocus` (keyboard and gamepad focus on nodes, as part of the tree: the arrows, the d-pad and the stick go to the nearest that way as drawn, what lies in line first, and of equally near ones the one focus last left; Tab and Shift Tab go in reading order, along rows the way `UI.ReadingDirection` reads, the current UI culture's unless set; a node's `FocusScope` makes it a section, which a move towards it enters as one, at the nearest inside or, with `FocusEntry` Remembered, where focus last was, and which Tab goes through before going on, as SwiftUI's focusSection, or a group, one stop until Submit enters it and Back leaves it, as Xbox's focus engagement; `PrefersFocus` says where focus starts; a presentation coming up takes focus, at what prefers it, and gives it back to what opened it as it goes; focus lost (hidden, disabled, gone) goes to the nearest where it was; a tap focuses a button, unseen; it shows only after a key, `Focused` on what has it and `FocusWithin` on everything it is in, and the first key after a pointer only shows it, where it is; meanwhile the focus ring is drawn round what has it, as iPadOS's focus halo, out past its edge and round its `ClipRadii`, in its root's look (`FocusRingColor`, `FocusRingWidth`, `FocusRingOffset`), `UINode.FocusRing` off on a node that shows focus its own way, and what takes focus in a scroll view is scrolled into view with room for it; and a first responder that has the keys while a field is edited) and `UIInput.Back`
- `TextField` (in `TimboJimbo.UINext.TextInput`, where UI Text Input is installed): text fields drawn by the stack and typed into with the platform's own keyboard through UI Text Input's `TextInputSystem`, with caret, selection, composition, secure fields, desktop shortcuts, mouse and touch selection, scrolling single lines and multi-line fields that grow
- `UINode.AvoidsKeyboard` and `UIRoot.KeyboardHeight`: content kept above the software keyboard, following it as it slides; the field being edited scrolls itself above the keyboard
- `ListView`: long lists that make only the rows near view, reusing cells as they scroll away, rows measuring themselves, what is in view kept put as rows above it are measured
- `Presentation`: sheets, alerts and pushed or full-screen pages in a stack, each laid out as a root of its own, with a dim backdrop, modality, back to dismiss, and culling below a cover while it is drawn in place (what is below is drawn again as it is swiped aside); presenting and dismissing each make all their state changes inside one change of their own and raise `Presenting` and `Dismissing` inside it, so code joins the change, as UIKit's animate(alongsideTransition:)
- `ScrollView.ScrollTo` inside a change scrolls on the change's animation, as part of it
- `DisplayTransition`: a node shown or hidden inside `UI.Animate` fades, grows or slides in and out
- Matched geometry, as SwiftUI's matchedGeometryEffect drawn as the web's view transitions draw it: `UINode.MatchName`, `MatchId`, which what is inside a node inherits, and `MatchAnchor`; inside one change, a node shown with the name and id of one the change hides takes over from it, flying from where that one was drawn to its own place (shown while a namesake stays, it flies out of it); one end is a picture of the hidden node as drawn, the other the shown node, live, cross-faded as one image by summing them, so nothing behind shows through where both are opaque; each end's own drawing (its box, border and shadow, an image filling it) is laid out at the flight's rect, and what is inside it is scaled evenly into the flight along the axis the two ends are most alike in, lined up along the other by its node's `MatchAnchor`, and cut to the flight's window, whose corners go from one end's `ClipRadii` to the other's; named parts inside an end fly on their own, drawn over it; what springs is the flight's difference from where its destination is drawn, so a scroll or a page sliding in carries it; drawn above everything at its level, out of clips; taken back mid-flight, it turns round from where it is drawn; it sets off at the speed its source was drawn moving
- `UINode.GroupOpacity`: while not fully opaque, a node and everything inside it is drawn to an image of its own first and faded as one, so overlapping parts don't show through each other; nested groups and clips work, each image covers only what its group draws, and fully opaque it costs nothing
- `UINode.Saturation` and `Brightness`: colour filters that multiply down the tree and cost nothing (applied to every pixel drawn, no image)
- `UINode.Mask`: a texture's alpha cuts a node and everything inside it, stretched over its rect (a layer, then masked)
- `UINode.Blur`: a node and everything inside it blurred as one image (dual Kawase passes on its group image), animated
- Content cross-fades: inside `UI.Animate`, a change to a drawn value that cannot interpolate (a label's text, an image's sprite, a drawable's bool or enum) fades the node's old drawing out as the new one fades in; changed again while fading, what is drawn then fades out
- A state's enter and exit animations move everything it changes when it is set outside `UI.Animate`, token values included
- `UIBuild`, extension methods for building trees from code
- Editor: the state bar, a Scene view overlay previewing interaction states, states and modes on the selected node (`UIStatePreview`), with a readout of the frame's draw calls, primitives and layers; inspector drawers for `UIValue<T>`, `Sizing`, `Insets` and `CornerRadii`; `UISimulator` and `UICapture`, which play and capture frames outside play mode
- Demos: Circle (`Demos/Scenes/Circle.unity`) and the showcase (`Demos/Scenes/Showcase.unity`), built by `Tools/UI Next/Demos`; in Circle the photo viewer flies out of the feed and home (thrown away any way from the photo or its backdrop, or pinched), a chat row grows into its conversation (its avatar and name becoming the header) and collapses back, stories grow out of their rings and shrink back, and Log out grows its dialog out of its button, which a drag anywhere throws away; the showcase's Store (a grid of items growing into a dialog, their icons, names and prices flying inside, which a drag anywhere throws away) and App Store (Today cards opening into stories, shrunk in place by a pull down past their top and closed back into the list, which scrolls the card into view), a stepped slider with momentum and one without, and a Focus page (a grid, sections side by side, a group, buttons that go, a row wider than the page, a dialog and a field to try keyboard and gamepad focus on, a switch that has rows read right to left, and a readout of where focus is); one demo component, `SwipeToDismiss`, behind every swipe to dismiss, moving its own node or a panel inside it, as far as the finger up to where letting go closes it and with a scroll view's rubber band past that
